# Epigraph Research

All quotes confirmed against Wikiquote and/or primary sources. Fabricated/misattributed quotes are
flagged and excluded. See per-section notes for attribution caveats.

## Full verified candidate pool

### Security / Authentication / Trust

**Phil Zimmermann — VERIFIED**
> "If privacy is outlawed, only outlaws will have privacy."

Source: "Why Do You Need PGP?" — *Why I Wrote PGP*, original PGP User's Guide, 1991; updated
version at The Ethical Spectacle, July 1995. Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Phil_Zimmermann`.

---

**Bruce Schneier — VERIFIED**
> "Security is a process, not a product."

Source: "The Process of Security", schneier.com, April 2000; expanded in *Secrets and Lies:
Digital Security in a Networked World* (Wiley, 2000). Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Bruce_Schneier`.

---

**Ronald Reagan — VERIFIED**
> "The maxim is: Dovorey no provorey — trust, but verify."

Source: Remarks at the Signing of the INF Treaty, East Room, The White House, December 8, 1987.
Transcript archived at the Miller Center and the Reagan Presidential Library.
The phrase derives from a Russian proverb (*Doveryai, no proveryai*) introduced to Reagan by
adviser Suzanne Massie.

---

**Whitfield Diffie & Martin Hellman — VERIFIED**
> "We stand today on the brink of a revolution in cryptography."

Source: Opening sentence of "New Directions in Cryptography", *IEEE Transactions on Information
Theory*, vol. 22, no. 6, pp. 644–654, November 1976. No dedicated Wikiquote page; verified
against the original publication record.

---

**Bruce Schneier — VERIFIED**
> "Complexity is the worst enemy of security."

Source: "A Plea for Simplicity: You Can't Secure What You Don't Understand", *Information
Security* magazine, November 1999. Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Bruce_Schneier`.

---

**Bruce Schneier — VERIFIED**
> "If you think technology can solve your security problems, then you don't understand the problems and you don't understand the technology."

Source: *Secrets and Lies: Digital Security in a Networked World* (Wiley, 2000), Preface.
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Bruce_Schneier`.

⚠ Schneier notes in the text that he adapted an earlier saying; the wording as quoted is his own.

---

**NOTE — Shannon's Maxim**
"The enemy knows the system" is widely attributed to Claude Shannon's 1949 Bell System Technical
Journal paper but is a later reformulation of Kerckhoffs's Principle by other commentators, not a
direct verbatim quotation from the Shannon paper. Do not use without access to the specific page.

### Guardrails / Safety / Vigilance

**Wendell Phillips — VERIFIED**
> "Eternal vigilance is the price of liberty — power is ever stealing from the many to the few."

Source: Speech to the Massachusetts Anti-Slavery Society, Boston, January 28, 1852. Published in
*Speeches Before the Massachusetts Anti-Slavery Society*, p. 13 (1853).
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Wendell_Phillips`.
Note: Phillips himself confirmed in an 1879 letter that the shorter form "Eternal vigilance is
the price of liberty" had been misattributed to Jefferson — the full version is his.

---

**Haile Selassie — VERIFIED**
> "Throughout history, it has been the inaction of those who could have acted, the indifference of those who should have known better, the silence of the voice of justice when it mattered most, that has made it possible for evil to triumph."

Source: University Graduation address, 2 July 1963. Published in *Important Utterances of H.I.M.
Emperor Haile Selassie I, 1963–1972* (1972), p. 22.
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Haile_Selassie`.
Note: Commonly but erroneously placed at his UN General Assembly speech of October 4, 1963 —
Wikiquote documents the July graduation address as the correct source.

---

**John Philpot Curran — VERIFIED**
> "The condition upon which God hath given liberty to man is eternal vigilance; which condition if he break, servitude is at once the consequence of his crime and the punishment of his guilt."

Source: Speech on the Right of Election of the Lord Mayor of Dublin, 10 July 1790. Published in
*The Speeches of the Right Honourable John Philpot Curran*, ed. Thomas Davis (1847), pp. 103–131,
at p. 105. Available on Wikisource.

---

**Benjamin Franklin — VERIFIED**
> "An ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure."

Source: "On Protection of Towns from Fire", *Pennsylvania Gazette*, 4 February 1735. Archived in
*The Papers of Benjamin Franklin*, vol. 2 (Yale UP, 1961); also at Founders Online (National
Archives). Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin` (Talk:Poor Richard's Almanack).
Note: The sentiment predates Franklin (Henry de Bracton, 13th century) but Franklin's 1735
articulation is the canonical modern form.

---

**Francis Bacon — VERIFIED**
> "The surest way to prevent seditions (if the times do bear it) is to take away the matter of them."

Source: *Essays*, "Of Seditions and Troubles" (1625). Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Essays_(Francis_Bacon)`. Available on Wikisource.

---

**REJECTED — Edmund Burke**
"All that is necessary for evil to triumph is that good men do nothing." — Spurious. No verified
primary source exists in Burke's writings. Disputed by Monticello research, multiple quote
scholars, and Wikiquote editors. Do not use.

**REJECTED — Marcus Aurelius** ("guard accordingly")
Widely cited to *Meditations* 4.3 but Wikiquote Talk page records editors disputing whether the
phrasing bears any relation to the Greek original. The translation is extremely liberal. Do not
use without citing a specific modern scholarly translation and line.

**REJECTED — Aristotle** ("mark of an educated mind")
Confirmed misquotation by Wikiquote editors and classical scholars. Earliest traceable
appearance is 1959. Do not use.

**REJECTED — Plato** ("price good men pay for indifference")
Widely circulated but not found in Plato's writings. Wikiquote Talk page notes this is
unsourced. Do not use.

### Observability / Logging / Measurement

**Lord Kelvin — VERIFIED**
> "…when you cannot measure it, when you cannot express it in numbers, your knowledge is of a meagre and unsatisfactory kind; it may be the beginning of knowledge, but you have scarcely, in your thoughts, advanced to the stage of science."

Source: Lecture "Electrical Units of Measurement", Institution of Civil Engineers, London,
3 May 1883. Published in *Popular Lectures and Addresses*, Vol. 1 (1889), pp. 80–81.
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/William_Thomson` and thematic `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Measurement`.
⚠ The popular short form "If you cannot measure it, you cannot improve it" does NOT appear in
the primary source. Use the verified form above (or an excerpt ending at "meagre and
unsatisfactory kind").

---

**Edsger W. Dijkstra — VERIFIED**
> "Program testing can be used to show the presence of bugs, but never to show their absence!"

Source: *Notes on Structured Programming* (EWD249), Section 3, p. 7, August 1969 / circulated
1970. Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Edsger_W._Dijkstra`. EWD249 available as PDF from the
University of Texas Dijkstra Archive.

---

**Donald Knuth — VERIFIED**
> "We should forget about small efficiencies, say about 97% of the time: premature optimization is the root of all evil. Yet we should not pass up our opportunities in that critical 3%."

Source: "Structured Programming with Goto Statements", *Computing Surveys* 6:4 (December 1974),
pp. 261–301, §1. Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Donald_Knuth`.
Note: Knuth later referred to this as "Hoare's Dictum" in 1989, but the attribution to Hoare
is considered doubtful per the Wikiquote talk page. Knuth's 1974 paper is the authoritative form.

---

**Richard Feynman — VERIFIED**
> "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself — and you are the easiest person to fool."

Source: "Cargo Cult Science", adapted from Feynman's 1974 Caltech commencement address; also
published in *Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!* (1985), p. 342.
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Richard_Feynman`.

---

**Tom DeMarco — VERIFIED**
> "You cannot control what you cannot measure."

Source: *Controlling Software Projects: Management, Measurement & Estimation* (1982), p. 3.
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Tom_DeMarco`.
Note: This is the correctly attributed version of the quote commonly (and wrongly) ascribed to
Drucker.

---

**W. Edwards Deming — VERIFIED (counter-quote)**
> "It is wrong to suppose that if you can't measure it, you can't manage it — a costly myth."

Source: *The New Economics for Industry, Government, Education* (1993), p. 35.
Wikiquote: thematic page `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Measurement`.
Useful if the chapter wishes to open with intellectual humility about over-reliance on metrics.

---

**REJECTED — Peter Drucker** ("If you can't measure it, you can't manage it")
Fabricated attribution. The Drucker Institute's archival research found no evidence Drucker ever
wrote or said this. The Deming Institute explicitly calls it a "costly myth". The Wikiquote
Drucker page does not contain this quote. Do not use.

---

### Routing / Request Pipelines

**Doug McIlroy — VERIFIED**
> "Make each program do one thing well. To do a new job, build afresh rather than complicate old programs by adding new 'features'. Expect the output of every program to become the input to another, as yet unknown, program."

Source: "UNIX Time-Sharing System: Foreword", *Bell System Technical Journal*, vol. 57, no. 6
(July–August 1978), pp. 1899–1904. Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Doug_McIlroy`.

---

**David Wheeler / Butler Lampson — VERIFIED with caveat**
> "All problems in computer science can be solved by another level of indirection."

Source: Attributed to David J. Wheeler; reported by Butler Lampson in his 1993 ACM Turing Award
Lecture. The "fundamental theorem of software engineering" label was added by Andrew Koenig.
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Computer_science` (thematic page).
⚠ No single Wheeler primary text pinned; the quote circulates through Lampson's retelling.
Attribute as: "David Wheeler (attr.), reported by Butler Lampson, ACM Turing Award Lecture, 1993."

---

**Edsger W. Dijkstra — VERIFIED**
> "It is what I sometimes have called 'the separation of concerns', which, even if not perfectly possible, is yet the only available technique for effective ordering of one's thoughts, that I know of."

Source: "On the role of scientific thought", EWD 447 (1974). University of Texas EWD Archive.
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Edsger_W._Dijkstra`.

---

**Edsger W. Dijkstra — VERIFIED**
> "The purpose of abstracting is not to be vague, but to create a new semantic level in which one can be absolutely precise."

Source: *Notes on Structured Programming*, EWD 249 (1970).
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Edsger_W._Dijkstra`.
⚠ Common misquotation substitutes "abstraction" for "abstracting". The Wikiquote page carries
the correct word.

---

### Configuration / System Design

**C.A.R. Hoare — VERIFIED (strongest provenance in this entire list)**
> "There are two ways of constructing a software design: One way is to make it so simple that there are obviously no deficiencies, and the other way is to make it so complicated that there are no obvious deficiencies. The first method is far more difficult."

Source: "The Emperor's Old Clothes", 1980 ACM Turing Award Lecture, published in *Communications
of the ACM*, 24(2), pp. 75–83 (February 1981).
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/C._A._R._Hoare`.

---

**Edsger W. Dijkstra — VERIFIED**
> "Simplicity is prerequisite for reliability."

Source: "How do we tell truths that might hurt?" (1975), reprinted in *Selected Writings on
Computing: A Personal Perspective* (Springer, 1982).
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Edsger_W._Dijkstra`.

---

**Edsger W. Dijkstra — VERIFIED**
> "Simplicity is a great virtue but it requires hard work to achieve it and education to appreciate it. And to make matters worse: complexity sells better."

Source: "On the nature of Computing Science", EWD 896 (1984).
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Edsger_W._Dijkstra`.

---

**Fred Brooks — VERIFIED**
> "Conceptual integrity is the most important consideration in system design. It is better to have a system omit certain anomalous features and improvements, but to reflect one set of design ideas, than to have one that contains many good but independent and uncoordinated ideas."

Source: *The Mythical Man-Month: Essays on Software Engineering* (Addison-Wesley, 1975), Chapter 4
("Aristocracy, Democracy, and System Design"). Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Fred_Brooks`.

---

**Alan Kay — VERIFIED (oral origin)**
> "Simple things should be simple, complex things should be possible."

Source: Oral statement at Xerox PARC, c. 1970s; confirmed by Kay in multiple talks and a Quora
response. Earliest print appearance: *ST.Mac* magazine, April 1984.
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Alan_Kay`.
⚠ No single canonical written-and-dated primary source. Flag as oral origin if citing formally.

### Rate Limiting / Restraint

**Epictetus — VERIFIED**
> "All philosophy lies in two words, sustain and abstain."

Source: Fragment of Epictetus recorded via Aulus Gellius, *Noctes Atticae* (Attic Nights), XIX.1.
Greek originals: Ἀνέχου (anekhou, "endure/sustain") and Ἀπέχου (apekhou, "abstain/refrain").
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Epictetus`.
⚠ The English rendering is a translator's phrase; the Aulus Gellius passage is the verified
anchor. Standard caveat for classical texts.

---

**Delphic Maxim — VERIFIED (ancient inscription)**
> "Nothing in excess." (Μηδὲν ἄγαν, mēdén ágan)

Source: Inscribed at the Temple of Apollo at Delphi. Cited by Plato, *Protagoras* 343b.
Associated with the Seven Sages of Greece.
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Seven_Sages_of_Greece` and `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Solon`.
⚠ Attribution to a single sage is historically ambiguous. Safe attribution: "Inscribed at
Delphi; cited by Plato, *Protagoras* 343b."

---

**Aristotle — VERIFIED (no crisp aphorism; use the scholarly condensation)**
> "Virtue is a mean between two vices, one of excess and one of deficiency."

Source: *Nicomachean Ethics*, Book II, Chapter 6 (1106b). Verbatim (Ross translation): "Virtue,
then, is a state of character concerned with choice, lying in a mean."
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Aristotle`.
Note: No single sentence from Aristotle reads like a modern aphorism on this theme. The
formulation above is a standard scholarly condensation; cite as "after *NE* II.6, tr. Ross."

### Budgets / Resource Management

**Seneca — VERIFIED**
> "People are frugal in guarding their personal property; but as soon as it comes to squandering time they are most wasteful of the one thing in which it is right to be stingy."

Source: *De Brevitate Vitae* (On the Shortness of Life), Chapter I, c. 49 AD.
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Seneca_the_Younger`.

---

**Adam Smith — VERIFIED**
> "Parsimony, and not industry, is the immediate cause of the increase of capital. Industry, indeed, provides the subject which parsimony accumulates."

Source: *An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations*, Book II, Chapter III
("Of the Accumulation of Capital"), 1776.
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Adam_Smith`.

---

**Benjamin Franklin — VERIFIED**
> "The way to wealth, if you desire it, is as plain as the way to market. It depends chiefly on two words, industry and frugality: that is, waste neither time nor money, but make the best use of both."

Source: *The Way to Wealth* (1758), preface to *Poor Richard's Almanack Improved*.
Project Gutenberg ID 43855. Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin`.

---

### Privacy / PII

**Warren & Brandeis — VERIFIED**
> "The right to life has come to mean the right to enjoy life, — the right to be let alone."

Source: Samuel D. Warren and Louis D. Brandeis, "The Right to Privacy", *Harvard Law Review*,
Vol. IV, No. 5 (Dec. 15, 1890), p. 193. Available via MIT CSAIL and Cornell Law.

---

**Louis Brandeis — VERIFIED**
> "The makers of our Constitution conferred, as against the Government, the right to be let alone — the most comprehensive of rights and the right most valued by civilized men."

Source: Dissent in *Olmstead v. United States*, 277 U.S. 438 (1928).
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Louis_Brandeis`.

---

**Louis Brandeis — VERIFIED**
> "Publicity is justly commended as a remedy for social and industrial diseases. Sunlight is said to be the best of disinfectants; electric light the most efficient policeman."

Source: "What Publicity Can Do", *Harper's Weekly*, Dec. 20, 1913; reprinted as Chapter V of
*Other People's Money — and How the Bankers Use It* (1914).
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Louis_Brandeis`.
⚠ The popular short form "Sunlight is the best disinfectant" drops the framing sentence.

---

**Edward Snowden — VERIFIED (contemporary figure)**
> "Arguing that you don't care about the right to privacy because you have nothing to hide is no different than saying you don't care about free speech because you have nothing to say."

Source: Reddit AMA, May 21, 2015; also in *Permanent Record* (2019).
Wikiquote: `en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden`.
Note: Contemporary figure — suitable only if the chapter tone allows modern voices.
